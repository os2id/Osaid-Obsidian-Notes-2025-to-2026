Cardio intensity is usually categorized into levels rather than by specific activities. Here's a simple breakdown:

| Level                     | Examples                                                          | Effort (1–10) | Can you talk?                       |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| **Low-intensity**         | Slow walking, easy cycling, casual swimming                       | 2–3           | Can hold a full conversation easily |
| **Moderate-intensity**    | **Brisk walking, power walking, light jogging**, moderate cycling | 4–6           | Can talk, but not sing              |
| **Vigorous-intensity**    | Running, fast cycling, swimming laps                              | 7–8           | Can only say a few words at a time  |
| **High-intensity (HIIT)** | Sprinting, hill sprints, assault bike intervals, burpees          | 9–10          | Talking is nearly impossible        |

So the activity **between walking and running** is typically called:
- **Brisk walking** (fast-paced walking)
- **Power walking**
- **Light jogging** (often considered the transition from walking to running)

If you're referring to the **intensity**, the term you're looking for is **moderate-intensity cardio** (also called **Zone 2–Zone 3 cardio**, depending on your heart rate).

Heart rate zones
- **Zone 1:** Very light (50–60% of max HR)
- **Zone 2:** Light to moderate (60–70%) – excellent for endurance and fat oxidation.
- **Zone 3:** Moderate (70–80%) – improves aerobic fitness.
- **Zone 4:** Hard (80–90%) – increases speed and performance.
- **Zone 5:** Maximum effort (90–100%) – sprints and all-out intervals.

If your goal is fat loss and general fitness:
A good progression is:
1. Walk
2. **Brisk walk / power walk**
3. Light jog
4. Run
5. Sprint
The "medium cardio" most people refer to is **brisk walking or light jogging**, which falls under **moderate-intensity cardio**.
